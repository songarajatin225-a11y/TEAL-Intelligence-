# Part XXII — The India Laser Ecosystem

India is strong in building and integrating laser machines for sheet metal, marking and diamond processing, but remains import-dependent for nearly every high-value subsystem: laser sources, pump diodes, scanners, precision optics, process heads and semiconductor-grade laser tools. New demand from ATMP/OSAT plants, cell gigafactories and electronics assembly — and the government’s shift in ISM 2.0 toward equipment and materials — creates the first sustained pull for localising those subsystems. Statements below are limited to what could be verified; company lists are illustrative, not exhaustive.

## 22.1 Map of the ecosystem

| Segment | Representative Indian actors | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Laser sources (industrial) | Research-scale development at RRCAT, Indore (Yb fiber lasers up to 50 W CW at 1096 nm; lamp-pumped industrial Nd:YAG lasers up to 500 W average) (RRCAT); industry–academia source programmes at an early stage | Import-dependent. Indian machine builders predominantly integrate imported sources; Chinese fiber sources are widely resold in India (IndiaMART listings) |
| High-energy lasers (defence) | DRDO CHESS, Hyderabad: 30 kW vehicle-mounted laser DEW Mk-II(A) demonstrated at Kurnool on 13 Apr 2025, with other DRDO labs and industry (DRDO) | Emerging; strategic demand driver for high-power fiber and beam-combining know-how |
| Laser diodes and pump modules | No volume high-power diode production identified | Import-dependent; long-term opportunity alongside compound-semiconductor fabs and ATMP capacity |
| Machine builders | SLTL Group, Gandhinagar (founded 1989; fiber cutting, marking, welding, diamond processing; >25,000 installations in 48+ countries per the company) (SLTL); many SMEs building cutting and marking machines around imported sources; international OEMs selling and servicing through Indian subsidiaries | Strong in sheet-metal cutting, marking and diamond/jewellery systems; weaker in semiconductor, display and ultrafast tools |
| Subsystems (scanners, heads, optics, diagnostics) | Largely imported; local fixture, enclosure and fume-extraction fabrication | Import-dependent; localisation opportunity for heads, fiber-cable assemblies, extraction, fixtures |
| System integrators | Automation and robot-cell integrators serving automotive, EMS and battery lines | Strong and growing; depth varies by domain |
| Research institutes | RRCAT (DAE), DRDO labs (CHESS, IRDE), CSIR labs (e.g., CSIO, CGCRI), IITs and IISc photonics, nanofabrication and laser-processing groups | Strong science base; limited translation into volume products |
| Semiconductor ecosystem (demand) | Micron ATMP, Sanand (inaugurated 28 Feb 2026); Kaynes Semicon, Sanand (inaugurated 31 Mar 2026); HCL–Foxconn JV (groundbreaking Feb 2026); Crystal Matrix compound-semiconductor fab and ATMP (Dholera) and Suchi Semicon OSAT (Surat) approved; 3D Glass Solutions packaging unit (Bhubaneswar) broken ground; SCL Mohali modernisation (₹4,500 crore); earlier approvals include the Tata Electronics fab (Dholera) and ATMP (Assam) and CG Semi OSAT (Sanand) (ISM) | Emerging; creates demand for wafer and package marking, dicing, singulation, laser-assisted bonding and — for glass substrates — through-glass-via processing |
| EMS ecosystem (demand) | Large and expanding smartphone and electronics assembly base | Strong demand for marking, depaneling, soldering, FPC cutting, camera-module and battery-pack joining |
| Battery ecosystem (demand) | Cell gigafactories under construction or ramping | Emerging demand for electrode notching, tab and busbar welding, can sealing, cleaning and marking |
| Automotive ecosystem (demand) | Large vehicle and component industry; EV transition under way | Strong demand for cutting, welding, brazing, hairpin and battery processes |
| Defence ecosystem | DRDO labs, defence PSUs, private defence manufacturers | Demand for rangefinders, designators, DEW sub-systems, UID marking |

ISM’s own timeline states that ISM 2.0 will focus on equipment and materials, design IP, supply chains and R&D centres (ISM) — the policy hook for Indian semiconductor laser-equipment programmes.

## 22.2 Strength, dependence, emergence and localisation

| Position | Areas |
|---|---|
| Strong | Sheet-metal cutting and marking machine building on imported sources; diamond and jewellery laser systems; system integration and field service; mechanical fabrication, enclosures, fixtures; HMI and application software; large price-sensitive domestic market |
| Import-dependent | Fiber, CO₂ (RF), UV and ultrafast sources; pump diodes; galvo scanners and controllers; F-theta and precision optics and coatings; process heads; beam diagnostics; precision motion (linear motors, air bearings); semiconductor laser tools (dicing, annealing, LLO) |
| Emerging | Indigenous fiber-source R&D; high-energy defence lasers; OSAT/ATMP and compound-semiconductor demand; battery gigafactories; laser application labs |
| Localisation opportunity | (1) Mid-power ns and CW fiber sources, initially with imported diodes; (2) marking, welding and cleaning machines tailored to EMS, battery and automotive lines with local service; (3) process heads, QBH-class cable assemblies, fume extraction and fixtures; (4) back-end semiconductor laser tools (package marking, singulation, glass-substrate processing) with technology partners; (5) diode packaging (ATMP) of imported chips into pump modules; (6) application labs and process-development services; (7) lifecycle services — repair, refurbishment, spares — for the installed base |

The localisation logic follows the value chain of Part XXI: start where Indian cost and service advantages are decisive (machines, heads, services), then move upstream into sources and diode packaging as volume and skills accumulate, partnering for the hardest components (epitaxy, ultrafast amplifiers, scanners).
