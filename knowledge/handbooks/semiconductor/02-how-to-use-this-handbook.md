# How to use this handbook

The semiconductor industry is one value chain with many branches: quartz becomes silicon, silicon becomes wafers, wafers become transistors, transistors become packaged ICs, and ICs become systems sold into end markets. Every chapter below locates itself on that chain.

The spine: raw materials to end market. Design (EDA, IP), equipment and materials feed in from the side at every stage.

## Three reading levels

| Level | Reader | What each chapter gives |
|---|---|---|
| L1 — Executive | Business leaders, investors, strategy, procurement | The concept and why it matters, readable in 3–5 minutes |
| L2 — Engineering | Product managers, engineers, manufacturing | Process flow, equipment, materials, parameters, defects |
| L3 — Deep dive | Process, equipment and device specialists | Physics, equations, equipment architecture, economics, trade-offs |

Each chapter opens with an L1 summary, carries the L2 body as its main text, and flags L3 material under its own sub-heading. Process chapters follow a fixed template: what it is → why it is needed → how it works → flow → equipment → materials → critical parameters → defects → quality metrics → suppliers → cost drivers → evolution → applications → localization → emerging technology.

## Accuracy conventions

Ranges, not false precision. Process parameters vary by node, tool and fab; the handbook gives typical ranges and says why they vary.

No invented market shares. Supplier lists name companies active in a segment; they are not rankings.

Status grading for projects. India and other capacity projects are tagged Operational · Under construction · Approved/announced · Proposed, with the date the status was checked.

Numbers carry context. Any market or capacity figure carries year, geography, definition, units and source. Revenue, market size, capacity, shipments and installed base are never mixed.

Illustrative models are labelled. Economics and scoring models state their assumptions explicitly.

Units. SI throughout (nm, µm, mm, °C, Pa/Torr noted where industry uses Torr, W, J/cm²).

Semiconductor ≠ electronics manufacturing. IC packaging (ATMP) and PCB assembly (EMS) are treated as distinct industries.

## Common misconceptions this handbook corrects

| Misconception | Reality |
|---|---|
| “3 nm” means a 3 nm transistor | Node names are marketing generations; no feature on a “3 nm” chip is 3 nm (Ch 17) |
| Semiconductors = advanced logic | Most units shipped are analog, discrete, power, sensor and mature-node MCUs on 180–28 nm |
| A fab makes the chip end-to-end | Front-end fab, back-end ATMP, and test are usually different companies and countries |
| Packaging is low-tech | Advanced packaging (CoWoS-class, hybrid bonding, HBM) is now a leading-edge bottleneck |
| The chip industry is only fabs | Equipment, materials, EDA/IP and OSAT are each strategic chokepoints |
